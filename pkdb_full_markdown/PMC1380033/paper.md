#### Interindividual and interethnic differences in the demethylation
### and glucuronidation of codeine

Department of Clinical Pharmacology, Karolinska Institute, Huddinge University Hospital, Sweden

1 The 8 h urinary excretion of codeine and seven of its metabolites was compared in 149
healthy Swedish Caucasians and 133 healthy Chinese following a single oral dose of 25 mg
codeine phosphate.
2 The total 8 h urinary recovery of drug-related material was 74 ± 24% in the Caucasians
and 60 ± 14% in the Chinese (P < 0.001). The excretion of unchanged codeine was
significantly higher in the Chinese (7.2%) compared with the Caucasians (4.3%, P < 0.001).
3 The Caucasians excreted significantly greater proportions of codeine-6-glucuronide
(C6G) (62%) than the Chinese (44%) (P < 0.001). The frequency distribution of the log
metabolic ratio (MR) for glucuronidation (codeine/C6G) was shifted towards higher
values in the Chinese population. Males in both groups and Chinese smokers had
significantly lower glucuronidation MRs than females and non-smokers in the respective
populations (P < 0.001).
4 The frequency distribution of the MR for O-demethylation (codeine/morphine (M) +
M-3 and M-6-glucuronide (M3G and M6G) + normorphine (NM) was highly skewed in
the Caucasians, suggestive of a bimodal distribution. There was a 160-fold interindividual
#### variation in this MR. A unimodal distribution of the log O-demethylation MR was

observed in Chinese. The Caucasians excreted less M and more M6G than did the Chinese

(P < 0.001).
5 Significantly more norcodeine (NC) and less NC-glucuronide (NCG) were excreted in
the Chinese compared with the Caucasians (P < 0.001). The mean value of the N#### demethylation MR was higher in Chinese (P < 0.001), with a 17-fold interindividual

variation in both populations.
6 The reproducibility of the indices of codeine metabolism was tested in 25 individuals.
The correlation coefficients between measurements taken on two occasions were 0.92, 0.77
and 0.79 for the MRs of 0- and N-demethylations and glucuronidation, respectively (P <
0.001). MRs measured in 8 h urine collections were similar to those in urine collected over
24h.
7 Pronounced interethnic differences were shown in the metabolism of codeine.
Compared with Caucasians, the Chinese were less able to metabolise codeine, in particular,
by glucuronidation. However, as observed with debrisoquine, the occurrence of genetically
poor 0-demethylators of codeine was rare in the Chinese compared with the Caucasians.

### Introduction
Codeine is an old and commonly-used analgesic
drug available world-wide. It is metabolised
mainly by conjugation with glucuronic <sup>acid,</sup>
although minor routes of metabolism involve 0demethylation to morphine (M) and N-demethylation to norcodeine (NC) (Figure 1, Adler <sup>et al.,</sup>
1955). It is safe to be given to healthy volunteers
and, therefore, may be regarded as a suitable
drug for conducting population studies of <sup>vari-</sup>
ability in glucuronidation. It also offers the
possibility of studying glucuronidation and
oxidation in the same individual. Four of <sup>the</sup>
codeine metabolites have analgesic effects
(Sanfilippo, 1948; Lasagne & De Kornfeld, <sup>1958;</sup>
Johannesson & Schou, 1963; Osborne et <sup>al.,</sup>

1988), i.e. M, morphine-6-glucuronide (M-6G), normorphine (NM) and NC. The fractions
of a dose of codeine converted to these metabolites have not been defined clearly in population
studies, and the possible occurrence of <sup>inter-</sup>
ethnic differences in codeine metabolism has not
been explored. The latter possibility <sup>is</sup> emphasised
by our recent finding that the 0-demethylation
of codeine co-segregates with the hydroxylation
of debrisoquine (Yue et al., 1989). It has been
shown previously that poor hydroxylators <sup>of</sup>
debrisoquine are rare in native Chinese population compared with Caucasians (Lou et al., <sup>1987).</sup>

In a recent editorial Kalow (1989) <sup>has</sup> <sup>underlined</sup>
the importance of studying <sup>inter-ethnic</sup> <sup>differ-</sup>
ences in drug metabolism and pharmacodynamics.

The aim of this study was <sup>to</sup> compare <sup>the</sup>
demethylation and glucuronidation <sup>of</sup> <sup>codeine</sup>
in Caucasians and Chinese.

A preliminary account of <sup>this</sup> paper <sup>was</sup>
presented in abstract form <sup>at</sup> <sup>the</sup> <sup>5th South</sup> <sup>East</sup>
Asian and Western Pacific Regional Meeting <sup>of</sup>
Pharmacologists (Yue et al., 1988).

The study was approved by the Ethics
Committee of Huddinge University Hospital.
Informed consent was obtained from all subjects.

Protocol

The subjects ingested a single oral dose of 25 mg
codeine phosphate (Kodein®, ACO) at bedtime
after emptying the bladder. Urine was collected
overnight for 8 h. The urine volume and pH were
measured and an aliquot was stored frozen at

-20° C until analysed.

In addition to the 8 h sample, six subjects (four
Caucasians and two Chinese) also collected urine
for 8-24 h after drug intake to study the effect of
the sampling period on metabolic ratios (MR).

The within-subject reproducibility of MRs
was investigated in 25 individuals (22 Caucasians
and three Chinese) who were re-phenotyped
with codeine at least 50 days after the first test.

The MRs for the three different metabolic
pathways were calculated as percent of dose
excreted as codeine divided by percent of dose
excreted as morphine (M), normorphine (NM),
morphine-3-glucuronide (M3G), morphine-6glucuronide (M6G), codeine-6-glucuronide
(C6G), norcodeine (NC) and norcodeine
glucuronide (NCG), respectively:

The proportions of codeine and its metabolites excreted in the urine during 8 h were
expressed as percentages of the given dose.

Chemicals

M6G was obtained from Ultrafine Chemicals,
Manchester, England. C6G and NC were supplied
by the National Institute of Drug Abuse, MD,
USA. Codeine was obtained from Apoteksbolaget, Stockholm, Sweden. For the sources of
all other chemicals see Svensson et al. (1982).

Assay

The method involved solid-phase extraction
(Sep-Pak C18, Waters) and ion-pair reversed
phase h.p.l.c. Detection was with a u.v.-detector
(M3G, NCG, C6G, NC and codeine) and a
coulometric electrochemical detector (M6G,
NM, M) coupled in series.

Modifications to the original method (Svensson
et al., 1982; Svensson, 1986) were as follows:

Sample clean-up: Urine (1 ml) was treated by
eluting codeine and its metabolites with 15%
v/v instead of 10% v/v acetonitrile. An aliquot
(100 p,1) of the eluate was injected into the h.p.l.c.

Chromatographic conditions: The eluent contained 31% v/v acetonitrile, instead of 26%.
Peak areas were measured with a LKB 2145
integrator.

Recoveries were > 85% for all compounds except
NM, for which the recovery was about 50%.

The r values for the standard curves were for
codeine (1 to 5,UM) 0.9979, for C6G (30 to
150 FIM) 0.9989, for M (1 to 5 AIM) 0.9999, for NM
(0.97 to 4.83 FIM) 0.9953, for M3G (4.1 to
20.5 FM) 0.9984, for M6G (0.17-0.86 FM) 0.9998,
and for NC (1 to 5,UM) 0.9985. The corresponding
values of the inter-assay coefficients of variation
(CV) for analyses were 8.3% (at a concentration
of 5 jiM), 8.0% (30 FIM), 6.3% (0.5 FM), 4.4%
(4.8 FM) and 2.8% (4.1 JIM), respectively (CV for
M6G and NC were not determined). The approximate minimum detectable concentrations were
10 nm for C6G, M, M3G and M6G and 20 nM
for codeine, NM and NC.

Because a reference sample of NCG was not
available, concentrations of this metabolite were
calculated using the standard curve for C6G.
Codeine and NC have similar u.v.-response and
standard curves (4% differences in the slope) so
it can be assumed that their glucuronides should
behave similarly. Moreover, the glucuronidation
of codeine has very little influence on
u.v.-response.

Statistical analysis

The unpaired Student's t-test and the MannWhitney U-test were used to compare parameters
between the two ethnic groups and the sex differences within each population. The effects of
smoking habits and the use of contraceptives
were also tested.

Correlation coefficients were calculated to
assess the reproducibility of the phenotyping test
(Spearman rank correlation was used for the
O-demethylation MRs). The effects of sampling
period, age, body-weight and volume of urine on
MRs were also analysed by this method.

Frequency histograms and probit plots (cumulative frequency distributions) were constructed
for the log MRs.

Biological reproducibility and different sampling
periods

The correlation coefficients for MRs of
O-demethylation, N-demethylation and
glucuronidation reactions were 0.92 (Spearman
Rank correlation, P < 0.001), 0.77 (P < 0.001)
and 0.79, (P < 0.001), respectively. MRs in 8 h
urine samples were similar to those in urine
collected over 24 h (ro-dem = 0.994, Spearman
Rank correlation; rN-dem = 0.994; rgluc = 0.996).

Urine volumes collected over 8 h ranged from
69-1061 ml and did not correlate with total drug
recovery or MRs. The urine pH varied between
5.1 and 8.1 and correlated directly with excreted
C6G (P < 0.05) and NCG (P < 0.01) and
inversely with codeine and the MRs (P < 0.001).
However, there was no significant difference in
urine pH between Caucasians and Chinese (6.0

+ 0.5 and 6.1 + 0.5, P > 0.05). No correlation
was found between the MRs and age or body
weight.

### Discussion
The Caucasians excreted significantly more C6G
and less codeine than the Chinese. Although the
Chinese who participated in this study had been
living in Sweden for a few weeks up to 8 years
almost all of them ate Chinese food and kept
Chinese habits, such as drinking tea instead of
coffee. It has been speculated that a low protein
intake in Africans might explain the finding of a
higher fractional recovery of the glucuronide
conjugate of paracetamol in this population
compared with Caucasians (Critchley et al.,
1986). Chinese usually consume less protein than
Caucasians but, nevertheless, excreted less C6G
in the present study. The possibility that Chinese
food contains less enzyme-inducing constituents
or substances that inhibit the glucuronidation of
codeine has to be considered. To what extent
environmental and genetic factors contribute to
the interethnic difference could not be assessed
from this population study. Further family or
twin studies are needed as well as more detailed
pharmacokinetic investigations of the fate of
codeine in man. In addition to slower metabolism,
other mechanisms may be responsible for the
significant differences in total recoveries between
the two populations, such as the existence of
unknown metabolic pathways in the Chinese
population.

Only a few studies have dealt with interethnic
differences in the glucuronidation of drugs.
Williams et al. (1980) compared the metabolic
fate of ethinyl oestradiol in different ethnic

groups; monoglucuronides predominated in the
urine of six American white women, diglucuronides predominated in the urine of 10 Nigerian
women, and 10 Sri Lankans excreted approximately equal proportions of the two. However,
the relevance of these differences is questionable
in view of the small numbers of subjects studied.
The conjugation of paracetamol has been investigated in different populations. It was found that
the clearance of paracetamol was, on average,
20% lower in 38 Asians than in 76 Europeans
(Mucklow et al., 1980). Critchley and co-workers
(1986) reported that Africans excrete more
paracetamol glucuronide than Caucasians, but
the difference was only 4% (54% compared with
58%). Glucuronidation is catalysed by a family
of closely-related enzymes which have different
but overlapping substrate specificities. The
existence of multiple forms of this enzyme has
been suggested (Siest et al., 1987). Thus, if two
populations are alike in the glucuronidation of
one drug, it does not necessarily follow that they
are alike in the glucuronidation of another drug.
Bock et al. (1987) advocated the use of paracetamol as a test drug to determine glucuronide
formation in man. Since 70 to 80% of an oral
dose of codeine is excreted in the form of C6G,
codeine should also be a suitable drug for testing
the capacity of glucuronidation in different
populations. In line with the results of Bock et al.
(1987) with paracetamol we also found indications
of induction of glucuronyltransferase by smoking.
An increased plasma clearance of codeine was
also observed in smokers (Rogers et al., 1982).
This phenomenon might be explained by an
increased glucuronidation rate due to smoking,
as indicated by the present results. Chinese males
had significantly lower glucuronidation MRs than
females. Similar sex differences have been
reported for paracetamol glucuronidation
(Miners et al., 1983). Since there was a larger
proportion of males and a higher average
glucuronidation MR in the Chinese population
compared with Caucasians, we might expect an
even greater difference if the two ethnic groups
were matched for sex.

The frequency distribution of the MR for
codeine O-demethylation appeared to be
bimodal in the Caucasian population. It has
been recently shown that the O-demethylation
of codeine co-segregates with debrisoquine
hydroxylation (Chen et al., 1988; Dayer et al.,

1988; Yue et al., 1989). Interestingly, only one
mode was found in the distribution of the codeine
O-demethylation MR in the Chinese. Debrisoquine has been the most commonly used pharmacogenetic probe in studies of drug oxidation,
although it is available in only a few countries

(Jackson, 1972). The frequency of slow (poor)
hydroxylators of debrisoquine has been tested in
different ethnic groups and large interethnic
differences have been observed. Poor hydroxylators of debrisoquine account for 5-10% of the
white population (Evans et al., 1980; Steiner et
al., 1985). By contrast, the frequency of poor
hydroxylators was found to be significantly lower
among Chinese (0.7% in 269 subjects) (Lou et
al., 1987) and Japanese (0% in 100 subjects)
(Nakamura et al., 1985). However, the criterion
of diagnosing a poor hydroxylator was based on
an antimode located at MR 12.6 originally
proposed by Evans et al. (1980) in Caucasians.
As discussed in detail by Steiner et al. (1985) this
antimode may not be valid for Orientals. We
have recently assessed drug hydroxylation
genotypes in some of our Chinese subjects
applying the restriction fragment length polymorphisms technique. Surprisingly, we found a
high percentage of Chinese subjects who were
genotypically poor hydroxylators and yet had
debrisoquine MRs well below 12.6. This indicates
that there is a dissociation between the results
obtained in phenotyping and genotyping tests in
Chinese possibly due to interaction between
genetic and environmental factors.

Interethnic differences in drug metabolism
will lead to corresponding differences in steady
state plasma drug concentrations and hence in
the effects of drugs prescribed in fixed doses
(Sjoqvist et al., 1980). It appears that Chinese
need smaller doses of codeine in the treatment of
pain since 15-30 mg/time is the recommended
dose (Jiang, 1984) compared with 25-50 mg/time
in Sweden. Therefore, doses which are suitable
for one ethnic population may not be appropriate
for another population. Furthermore, Zhou et
al. (1989) have reported that Chinese subjects
are more sensitive to propranolol despite the
fact that they metabolised the drug more efficiently than their Caucasian counterparts. The
latter finding is of interest since these Chinese
subjects had adopted Western dietary habits.
Further pharmacokinetic and pharmacodynamic
studies are needed to explore the clinical relevance
of our findings with codeine.
