## Abstract

**Purpose:** 
This study aimed to establish a population pharmacokinetic model and optimise tacrolimus dosing regimens in Chinese lung transplant recipients.

**Methods:** 
A total of 988 tacrolimus trough concentrations and clinical data of 142 adult lung transplant recipients were collected. Population pharmacokinetic analysis was performed using a nonlinear mixed effects model. A Monte Carlo simulation was conducted to determine the optimal dosing regimen.

**Results:** 
The pharmacokinetics of tacrolimus could be best described by a one-compartment model with first-order absorption and elimination. The typical population parameter estimates of apparent clearance and apparent volume of distribution were 7.58 L·h−1 and 701.39 L, respectively. The clearance of tacrolimus in rapid and intermediate metabolisers of CYP3A5 was 2.72-fold and 1.87-fold higher, respectively, than in poor metabolisers of CYP3A5. The concurrent use of voriconazole, posaconazole, and itraconazole led to a reduction in tacrolimus clearance by 38.21%, 26.30%, and 57.98%, respectively. Recommended dose regimens were obtained by Monte Carlo simulation based on the established model.

**Conclusion:** 
Recipients with the CYP3A5*3/*3 genotype, elevated haematocrit levels, short postoperative days, and concurrent administration of azole antifungal drugs needed a reduced maintenance dose to reach the therapeutic window, which provided a reference for the formulation of individualised tacrolimus regimen during the early post-transplantation phase.

Keywords: Lung transplantation, Tacrolimus, Population pharmacokinetics, Nonlinear mixed effects model, Monte Carlo simulation

## Introduction

Tacrolimus is a potent calcineurin inhibitor that is widely used to prevent rejection in lung transplant recipients and plays a key role in immune maintenance therapy. Compared with other immunosuppressive agents, tacrolimus shows better efficacy and safety in recipients with lung transplants and is often used in combination with mycophenolic acids and glucocorticoids, which is the cornerstone drug of the immunosuppressive regimen after lung transplantation [[1](#CR1), [2](#CR2)].

However, the therapeutic window of tacrolimus is narrow, and the individual variation of pharmacokinetics is large, which restricts the clinical application of tacrolimus. The narrow therapeutic window affects the effectiveness and safety of anti-rejection treatment after lung transplantation. Insufficient drug concentration elevates the risk of rejection and excess drug concentration may lead to adverse events such as infection and renal injury [[3](#CR3), [4](#CR4)]. Due to a variety of confounding factors such as genetic factors, pathophysiological factors, and combined medication, the blood level of tacrolimus is highly unstable and variable, making it difficult for clinicians to develop reasonable individualised dosing regimens [[5](#CR5), [6](#CR6)].

Therapeutic drug monitoring (TDM) provides a quantitative basis for the adjustment of tacrolimus dosage which is based on the correlation between tacrolimus blood concentration and clinical efficacy. Whole blood trough concentration of tacrolimus is regularly monitored within the recommended range of 10–15 ng·mL^−1^ within the first 3 months after lung transplantation [[7](#CR7), [8](#CR8)]. Although tacrolimus TDM has been widely used in the clinic, its application has limitations. First, the recommended tacrolimus window for lung transplantation above still lacks solid evidence from large clinical trials or authoritative guidelines, which now largely refer to those of other solid organ transplantation populations. In addition, in the early stage after lung transplantation, cardiopulmonary function is unstable, and various organs experience different degrees of ischemia and hypoxia during operation, combined with organ dysfunction, resulting in large fluctuations in tacrolimus trough concentration.

CYP3A5 is the main metabolic enzyme of tacrolimus, which significantly affects the metabolic process of tacrolimus. Many studies have shown that the CYP3A5*3 polymorphism (rs776746) has a significant effect on the blood concentration of tacrolimus [[9](#CR9)–[11](#CR11)]. The Clinical Pharmacogenetics Implementation Consortium recommends that recipients with extensive and medium metabolism adopt 1.5–2 times the standard dose [[12](#CR12)]. The mutation rate of the CYP3A5*3 locus is significantly different in different ethnic groups, especially in the Asian population with a higher mutation rate of 74.2% [[9](#CR9)]. Even if the dosage is formulated according to the CYP3A5 polymorphism, due to the pharmacokinetic characteristics of tacrolimus and the influence of concomitant drugs, TDM is still needed for dosage adjustment.

The study of the population pharmacokinetics of tacrolimus in the lung transplantation population will help explore the individual differences of drugs. However, at present, there are few studies in this field [[13](#CR13)]. The concentration–time curve of tacrolimus in recipients 1 week after lung transplantation showed a high degree of variability, and the blood concentration of 69% of cases exceeded the target range at 12 h [[14](#CR14)]. The pharmacokinetic process of tacrolimus differ between recipients with cystic fibrosis and non-cystic fibrosis [[15](#CR15)]. Many factors, such as CYP3A5*3 genotype, primary disease, haematocrit, body weight, and combined medication have an impact on the clearance rate of tacrolimus, resulting in individual differences in blood concentration [[16](#CR16), [17](#CR17)]. The above studies show that the blood concentration of tacrolimus fluctuates largely after lung transplantation. It is necessary to further explore the factors that influence individual differences in tacrolimus after lung transplantation. However, cystic fibrosis is a rare disease in Asia, and its results are not suitable for the Chinese population. Furthermore, there is limited study in the Chinese population, and the lack of CYP3A5*1/*1 data cannot provide an optimised administration regimen for postoperative maintenance dose in Chinese or other Asian lung transplant recipients [[17](#CR17)].

Based on the presented findings, additional TDM and CYP3A5 genotype-based pharmacokinetics research is warranted for early lung transplant recipients, with the objective of furnishing a more robust foundation and precise personalized treatment strategies. This study used a nonlinear mixed effect model (NLME) to analyse influencing factors of individual variables of pharmacokinetics after lung transplantation in the Chinese population. Combined with Monte Carlo simulation, a population pharmacokinetic model of tacrolimus was established to optimise the dosing regimen of tacrolimus in Chinese lung transplant recipients.

## Methods

### Subjects and data collection

The study was conducted in accordance with the Basic & Clinical Pharmacology and Toxicology policy for experimental and clinical studies [[18](#CR18)]. This research received approval from by the Ethics Committees of the The First Affiliated Hospital of Guangzhou Medical University (ES-2023-K010). Informed consent was obtained from all participants included in the study.

One hundred and forty-two subjects underwent lung transplantation at the First Affiliated Hospital of Guangzhou Medical University from January 2019 to December 2021were enrolled in this analysis. The inclusion criteria were as follows: (1) Chinese recipients who underwent lung transplantation for the first time; (2) recipients who received immunosuppressive maintenance therapy based on tacrolimus after surgery; and (3) recipients receiving tacrolimus treatment drug monitoring within three months after lung transplantation. The exclusion criteria were as follows: (1) recipients with combined organ transplantation; (2) recipients with severe systemic complications and hemodynamic dysfunction, liver dysfunction (Child–Pugh C) or renal function damage (creatinine clearance rate less than 15 ml/min); (3) recipients with poor compliance and inability to take medicine on time during the observation period; and (4) recipients with incomplete clinical situation and medical records during the observation period.

### Administration regimens

The anti-rejection treatment plan comprised triple immunotherapy, and the main treatment drugs included tacrolimus capsule (Prograf, Astellas Pharma); mycophenolate mofetil tablets (MMF, Roche Pharmaceutical); mycophenolate sodium enteric-coated tablets (Myfortic, Novartis Pharmaceutical); and glucocorticoids (prednisone, methylprednisolone). The initial dose of tacrolimus was 50–150 μg·kg^−1^·d^−1^, administered every 12 h. The dose was adjusted according to the trough concentration monitoring value, and the target trough concentration was controlled at 10–15 ng·mL^−1^ [[8](#CR8)]. In addition, none of the patients enrolled in this study received caspofungin, Wuzhi capsule, macrolide antibiotics and calcium channel blocker that affected tacrolimus concentration.

### General clinical data

(1) General data: age, sex, height, weight, postoperative days (POD), main indications, and types of transplantation; (2) laboratory indexes during treatment: white blood cell count (WBC), red blood cell count (RBC), platelet count (PLT), haematocrit (HCT), haemoglobin (HB), alanine aminotransferase (ALT), aspartate aminotransferase (AST), total bilirubin (Tbil), albumin (ALB), total protein (TP), creatinine (Cr); (3) combined medication situation: glucocorticoid (GC), mycophenolic acid (MPA), azole antifungal drugs (AFDs), and proton pump inhibitor (PPI).

### Blood concentration detection

After tacrolimus reached a steady state (approximately 48 h after first tacrolimus dose), 1–2 mL ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid (EDTA) anticoagulant blood samples were collected 30 min before medication, and the whole blood concentration of tacrolimus was detected by chemiluminescence microparticle immunoassay (CMIA) [[19](#CR19)]. The instrument used for quantification was ARCHITECT i1000SR (Abbott, Chicago, IL, USA), and the reagents were the Abbott 1L77 tacrolimus detection kit, 1L77 tacrolimus sample pre-treatment reagent, and supporting quality control and calibration solution (Abbott, Chicago, IL, USA). The quantification was done as manufacturer’s instructions.

### Genotype detection

In this study, 1–2 mL EDTA anticoagulant was collected. Genomic DNA was extracted using a blood genomic extraction kit. The CYP3A5 6986 A > G genotype was detected by polymerase chain reaction using the polymerase chain reaction-restriction fragment length polymorphism (PCR–RFLP) method [[20](#CR20)]. The instruments used for genotyping were a PCR amplification instrument (ABI 9700; Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, MA, USA), electrophoresis apparatus (DYY-6D; LIUYI, Beijing, China), and gel imager (ZF-258; JIAPENG, Shanghai, China).

### Data statistics

SPSS software (Version 22.0; IBM, Armonk, NY, USA) was used for statistical analysis, with *P* < 0.05 as the significant difference: (1) The genotype and allele frequencies of each point were calculated, and the chi-square test was used to analyse whether the distribution of each genotype was in accordance with Hardy–Weinberg equilibrium; (2) Spearman correlation analysis was used to analyse the correlation between the two groups of quantitative data, and Mann–Whitney *U* test was used to analyse the correlation between quantitative data and binary variables; the correlation between quantitative data and multi-categorical variables was analysed using the Kruskal–Wallis H test.

### Pharmacokinetic analysis

Phoenix NLME software (Version 8.2; Certara, Princeton, NJ, USA) was used for population pharmacokinetic analysis, and R software (Version 4.0.4; R Foundation, Vienna, Austria) was used for auxiliary mapping and visual evaluation.

### Establishment of basic model

The tacrolimus blood concentration data are trough concentrations; therefore, it is difficult to estimate the oral bioavailability and absorption delay time. According to a comprehensive literature report [[13](#CR13)], the absorption rate constant (Ka) is fixed at 4.48 h^−1^, and the clearance (CL/F) and apparent distribution volume (V_d_/F) include the bioavailability of oral preparations. One- and two-compartment models were used to optimise the combination of multiple residual and atrioventricular models. The objective function value (OFV) was taken as -2 log likelihood (-2LL) and the optimal basic model was selected according to the OFV and goodness-of-fit plots.

The inter-individual variation of pharmacokinetic parameters is described by the exponential model (Eq. [1](#Equ1)), and the intra-individual variation by the additive, proportional, exponential, and mixed proportional-additive models.

| Pi=θi×expηi | 1 |
| --- | --- |
This study assumes that the residual variation η obeys a normal distribution with a mean of 0 and a variance of ω^2^, P_i_ is the pharmacokinetic parameter of the i-th subject, θ_i_ is the typical value of the PK parameter.

### Model development

A single factor analysis of covariates and pharmacokinetic parameters was performed. Covariates with significant correlations were included in the model according to the fitting degree of the covariate box diagram. Stepwise regression was used to screen the covariates, and they were filtered through forward inclusion and backward elimination. When the introduced covariate reduced the -2LL value by 3.84, it was included as a covariate. When the eliminated covariate increased the -2LL value by less than 6.64, the covariate was eliminated. The model was optimised until no significant change in the value of the objective function was found, and finally, the full model was obtained.

Continuous variables were converted using a median-corrected power function model (Eq. [2](#Equ1)), and categorical variables were converted using an exponential model (Eq. [3](#Equ3)).

| θi=θ×COViCOVmedianθCOV | 2 |
| --- | --- |
| θi=θ×expθcovi | 3 |
| --- | --- |
Among them, COV_i_ is the i-th covariate, COV_median_ is the median value of the covariate, θ_i_ is the population prediction value of pharmacokinetic parameters, and θ_COVi_ is the fixed effect value of this covariate on pharmacokinetic parameters.

According to the above model, inter-individual variation, residual error, and intra-individual variation were adjusted and optimised. The minimum value of the objective function between different models was compared, and the optimal model was selected as the final model.

### Model evaluation and validation

Goodness-of-fit plots, visual predictive check (VPC), and bootstrap were used to internally verify the final model: Goodness-of-fit plots included observed concentration (OBS) versus individual predicted concentration (IPRED), observed concentration versus population predicted concentration (PRED), conditional weighted residuals (CWRES) versus time and CWRES versus PRED; VPC was applied to test the prediction performance of the final model by 1000 simulated data sets. The concentration–time curves of tacrolimus were plotted for the 5th, 50th, and 95th percentiles of the simulated concentration; Bootstrap generates simulation data sets by sampling the original data for 1000 times to evaluate the stability and accuracy of the final model. The median values of parameters and the 95% confidence interval (CI) of parameter estimates obtained from the bootstrap were compared with the estimates of the final model.

### Monte Carlo simulation

Based on the above final model, Monte Carlo simulation was run for different dosing schemes under different covariates, and 1000 times were simulated for each case. Steady-state trough concentrations of tacrolimus were determined in patients with varying CYP3A5 genotypes and concomitant AFDs, at median HCT and POD values, as well as in patients with distinct HCT and POD profiles within the CYP3A5 *1/*3 genotype, with no AFDs concomitant medication.

## Discussion

Tacrolimus is a basic drug used for immune maintenance treatment after lung transplantation. However, its therapeutic window is narrow and exhibits significant inter-individual variability, rendering the development of a rational, individualized dosing regimen challenging. Consequently, investigating the factors contributing to these pharmacokinetic and pharmacodynamic differences in tacrolimus disposition is crucial to provide a basis for adjusting the drug regimen. This study was aimed at the early population after lung transplantation. By analysing the blood concentration of tacrolimus and relevant clinical data, the pharmacokinetic characteristics of tacrolimus in the Chinese lung transplant population were discussed using the NLME model. Finally, the CYP3A5*3 genotype, HCT, POD, and AFDs were selected in the final model, and combined with Monte Carlo simulation, the postoperative maintenance dose under different conditions was optimised, which can provide a reference basis for the formulation and adjustment of tacrolimus dose in early recipients after lung transplantation.

In the previous population pharmacokinetic studies of tacrolimus, 81% of the studies based on trough concentration data were constructed using a one-compartment model [[21](#CR21)]. For the intra-individual variation residual model, 35.9% of the studies used the proportional model, 26.6% used the additive model, and 20% chose the mixed proportional-additive model. Tacrolimus blood concentration data collected in this study were trough concentrations. The final model was a one-compartment model with first-order elimination based on fitting results and optimisation of various models. The inter-individual and intra-individual variation adopted exponential and a mixed proportional-additive model, respectively. In addition, the model had only trough concentration data and no sampling in the absorption stage. Therefore, based on the literature report, the absorption rate constant (Ka) was fixed at 4.48 h^−1^, and 74% of relevant studies selected fixed parameters to build the model [[13](#CR13)]; the final model clearance (CL) and volume of distribution (V) in this study were 7.58 L·h^−1^ and 701.39 L, respectively, with the observed CL being lower than that reported in relevant studies [[22](#CR22)]. Several factors could be applied to explain the discrepancy between our study results and that in previous studies. Firstly, the subjects in our study were generally older patients, with a mean age of 57 years (much older than 50 years old reported in other studies), and there is a prone for tacrolimus to be easily accumulated in the elderly population, leading to a relatively lower clearance rate of this medication [[23](#CR23)]; Secondly, our study subjects had a comparatively lower body weight, which means that tacrolimus was distributed in a smaller body surface area, resulting in a lower clearance.

Genetic factors are the key to individual differences in tacrolimus and are an important factor guiding the dosage of tacrolimus [[24](#CR24)]. The final model of the present study included the CYP3A5*3 genotype, which also had the greatest effect on the OFV model in the process of screening covariates by stepwise regression method (−84.92), indicating that this factor had a significant impact on the clearance of tacrolimus. CYP3A5 is the main metabolic enzyme involved in tacrolimus. Previous studies based on lung transplantation populations also found that the CYP3A5*3 genotype has an important impact on tacrolimus metabolism [[9](#CR9), [11](#CR11), [17](#CR17), [25](#CR25), [26](#CR26)]. This study found that the tacrolimus clearance rates of the CYP3A5 rapid metabolic (*1/*1) and intermediate metabolic (*1/*3) populations were 2.72 times and 1.87 times higher than that of the CYP3A5 poor metabolic (*3/*3) population, respectively. The study by CAI et al. indicated that the tacrolimus clearance of the CYP3A5 gene intermediate metabolic type (*1/*3) population was 1.30 times higher than that of the CYP3A5 poor metabolic type (*3/*3) population, and no other studies related to the lung transplant population have been reported [[17](#CR17)]. In this study, the clearance rates among different genotypes are different, which further expands the study of the fast-metabolic population, implying that the drug dose needs to be further distinguished and adjusted among different metabolic types in the lung transplantation population, and the fast-metabolic recipients need a higher dose to achieve the target therapeutic window. In addition, other studies have investigated the impact of CYP3A4 and other CYP3A5 genotype on the pharmacokinetics of tacrolimus. However, in Asian patients, the occurrence frequencies of CYP3A4*1B, CYP3A4*22, CYP3A5*7, and CYP3A5*7 were all below 1%, indicating that their roles in our population were limited. [[27](#CR27), [28](#CR28)].

Haematological indexes are also important factors that lead to individual differences in tacrolimus. This study found that HCT was negatively correlated with clearance. When HCT decreased from 0.30 to 0.20, tacrolimus clearance elevated by 33.36%. The correlation between HCT and tacrolimus clearance has been reported in many studies; 28.6% of tacrolimus population pharmacokinetic studies have discussed the relationship between HCT and clearance [[21](#CR21)]. Tacrolimus is highly combined with RBCs. Recipients with low levels of HCT have more free tacrolimus in the plasma, resulting in a corresponding increase in clearance[[29](#CR29), [30](#CR30)]. Some studies have suggested that HCT causes individual differences in the whole blood concentration of tacrolimus in the lung transplant population. When the HCT decreases from 0.45 to 0.30, the unbound tacrolimus plasma concentration of tacrolimus is 1.5 times higher [[31](#CR31)]; In another study of lung transplant populations, HCT also showed a negative correlation with clearance, and the typical value of the population was −0.868 [[17](#CR17)], which aligns with our findings.

The effect of postoperative days on tacrolimus concentration has been reported in several studies, especially in early postoperative recipients, revealing an observed increase in tacrolimus clearance over time following transplantation [[32](#CR32), [33](#CR33)]. Our study further supports this finding by demonstrating a significant elevation of 16.63% in tacrolimus clearance between the 30-day and 90-day periods after transplantation. It may be attributed to the gradual restoration of gastrointestinal motility and CYP3A enzyme activity during the early postoperative phase.

Drug combination is a potential factor affecting the blood concentration of tacrolimus, and azole antifungal drugs are commonly used in lung transplantation recipients to prevent or treat invasive fungal infections. As a powerful CYP3A enzyme and P-glycoprotein inhibitor, it has a significant interaction with tacrolimus. In our study, the effects of three AFDs on tacrolimus clearance were systematically analysed and compared. Voriconazole decreased tacrolimus clearance by 38.21%, posaconazole led to a 26.30% slower clearance, and itraconazole reduced the clearance by 57.98%. A negative correlation between AFDs and tacrolimus clearance has been reported. For the study on lung transplant recipients, one indicated that voriconazole led to a 36.2% slower clearance for tacrolimus [[17](#CR17)]; another study show that dose-corrected trough concentrations (C/D) tripled in patients receiving posaconazole [[34](#CR34)]; and the mean increase in the total daily dose of tacrolimus after itraconazole withdrawal was 76% compared with 64% for voriconazole [[35](#CR35)]. In a multicentre study of solid organ recipients, tacrolimus C/D increased more for voriconazole than for posaconazole [[36](#CR36)]. The difference in the effect of AFDs on tacrolimus clearance may be due to the difference in the inhibition of CYP3A4. At equimolar concentrations, ketoconazole is the most potent CYP3A4 inhibitor, followed by itraconazole, voriconazole, and fluconazole [[37](#CR37)]. And itraconazole is the most lipophilic azole which is prone to enzymatic and transporter-mediated interactions [[38](#CR38)]. Moreover, the patients enrolled in our study mainly used posaconazole as suspension, which shows smaller impact than delayed-release tablets [[39](#CR39)].Above all, when using these drugs, the dosage of tacrolimus should be reduced accordingly.

In addition, weight, age, and renal function indexes might affect the drug concentration of tacrolimus, which needs to be further discussed [[40](#CR40), [41](#CR41)].

In this study, we employed Monte Carlo simulation to simulate and predict the drug concentration under various dose conditions, considering different covariates. The results highlight the necessity for reduced maintenance doses in CYP3A5 poor metabolic recipients, postoperative recipients with elevated HCT values or short POD, and AFDs co-medication to achieve the desired therapeutic window. An increasing number of population pharmacokinetic studies carry out simulation predictions according to covariate results included in the final model, and individualised dose adjustments are made for patient groups under different conditions. These simulation results may be disturbed by the research population and confounding factors, and need to be included in more comprehensive samples and variables for further analysis and adjustment.

Presently, the majority of research on tacrolimus population pharmacokinetics is primarily focused on kidney and liver transplantation, with a striking absence of studies investigating its application in lung transplantation populations. It is necessary to continue to explore and summarise the population pharmacokinetic characteristics of this population. In addition, studies on the application of tacrolimus sustained release formulation and intra-individual variability will also provide new ideas for the study of population pharmacokinetics and assist in the individualised and accurate application of tacrolimus in early recipients after lung transplantation [[42](#CR42), [43](#CR43)].

## Conclusions

A population pharmacokinetic model of tacrolimus in Chinese lung transplant recipients was successfully constructed and evaluated, with the CYP3A5 genotype, HCT, POD, and AFDs as significant covariates. The recipients with the CYP3A5*3/*3 genotype, elevated HCT levels, short POD, and concurrent AFD administration require reduced maintenance doses to achieve the target therapeutic window, which provided a reference for the formulation of individualised tacrolimus regimen during the early post-transplantation phase.
