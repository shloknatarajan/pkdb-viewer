## Abstract

**PURPOSE:** 
Belzutifan is a novel treatment for von Hippel-Lindau–associated cancers and is being evaluated in clinical trials for the treatment of renal cell carcinoma in combination with the cyclin-dependent kinase 4/6 inhibitors abemaciclib (ClinicalTrials.gov identifier: NCT04627064) and palbociclib (ClinicalTrials.gov identifier: NCT05468697). Belzutifan is metabolized via CYP2C19 and UGT2B17 and is a weak inducer of CYP3A. Induction of CYP3A is predicted to have a greater effect in dual CYP2C19/UGT2B17 poor metabolizers (PMs) relative to normal metabolizers (referred to as extensive metabolizers [EMs] in Simcyp). Our objective was to assess the drug-drug-gene interaction (DDGI) potential of belzutifan when coadministered with the CYP3A substrates abemaciclib and palbociclib in individuals with varying CYP2C19/UGT2B17 phenotypes.

**METHODS:** 
Physiologically based pharmacokinetic (PBPK) models for belzutifan, abemaciclib, and palbociclib were constructed in Simcyp v.21 and confirmed using data from US Food and Drug Administration reviews and previous publications. Virtual trials evaluating the area under the concentration-time curve from time 0 to infinity (AUC0-inf) of single-dose palbociclib (125 mg) or abemaciclib (200 mg) alone and after 7 days of belzutifan (120 mg once daily) were simulated in healthy volunteers with differing CYP2C19/UGT2B17 phenotypes.

**RESULTS:** 
Compared with dual CYP2C19/UGT2B17 EMs, predicted belzutifan exposure increased approximately 4.4-fold in dual PMs. Belzutifan decreased the AUC0-inf of abemaciclib by 24.3% and 52.2% in dual EMs and dual PMs, respectively, with the relative potency-adjusted unbound AUC0-inf decreasing by 23.5% and 47.9%. Similarly, belzutifan decreased the AUC0-inf of palbociclib by 13.2% and 36.8% in dual EMs and dual PMs, respectively.

**CONCLUSION:** 
Our simulations predict that belzutifan decreases the exposure of combination therapies metabolized via CYP3A, with the severity of interaction dependent on CYP2C19 and UGT2B17 phenotypes.

## INTRODUCTION

Belzutifan is a novel treatment option for several von Hippel-Lindau (VHL)–associated cancers, including renal cell carcinoma (RCC), central nervous system hemangioblastomas, and pancreatic neuroendocrine tumors.^1,2^ Belzutifan received priority review and approval on August 13, 2021, on the basis of interim results from the Phase II MK-6482–004 trial.^3^ It is now being studied beyond its initial germline VHL indication to treat advanced RCC more broadly, both alone, and in combination with other therapies, including immune checkpoint inhibitors, tyrosine kinase inhibitors, and cyclin-dependent kinase (CDK) 4/6 inhibitors.^4,5^

Belzutifan is primarily metabolized via CYP2C19 and UGT2B17, with dual poor metabolizers (PMs) predicted to have 3.2-fold higher steady-state area under the concentration-time curve (AUC) values compared with normal metabolizers (referred to as extensive metabolizers [EMs] in Simcyp) of both pathways.^6^ Notably, belzutifan is also a weak inducer of CYP3A, on the basis of the results of a clinical drug-drug interaction (DDI) study showing belzutifan 120 mg once daily for 7 days decreased the AUC ratio (AUCR) of the CYP3A clinical index substrate midazolam by 40% (MK6482–009).^7^ As greater CYP3A induction would be expected in patients with higher belzutifan concentrations, a previous physiologically based pharmacokinetic (PBPK) model was used to simulate the midazolam AUCR in CYP2C19/UGT2B17 PMs, predicting up to a 70% decrease in dual PMs.^7^

Expanding on their initial breast cancer indication, there is an evolving interest in studying CDK 4/6 inhibitors to treat other types of solid tumors, including RCC, both alone and in combination with other therapies.^8,9^ CDK 4/6 inhibitors offer a compelling option with inhibitors of HIF-2α, with in vitro and mice xenograft models showing potential synthetic lethality when used in combination.^10^ Accordingly, two clinical trials are studying belzutifan in combination with the CDK4/6 inhibitors abemaciclib ([ClinicalTrials.gov](http://ClinicalTrials.gov) identifier: [NCT04627064](https://clinicaltrials.gov/ct2/show/NCT04627064))^11^ and palbociclib ([ClinicalTrials.gov](http://ClinicalTrials.gov) identifier: [NCT05468697](https://clinicaltrials.gov/ct2/show/NCT05468697)).^12^ Since abemaciclib and palbociclib are primarily metabolized via CYP3A, with both US Food and Drug Administration (FDA) labels issuing warnings against the use of concomitant CYP3A inducers because of the potential for reduced therapeutic benefit,^13,14^ it is plausible that this combination may be compromised in certain populations (ie, dual PMs). For abemaciclib, accurate characterization of CYP3A induction must consider numerous metabolites (ie, metabolite 2 [M2], metabolite 18 [M18], and metabolite 20 [M20]) that are formed by CYP3A and are relatively equipotent to the parent compound.^13,15^ Nevertheless, past DDI studies with strong and moderate CYP3A inducers reduced the potency adjusted unbound AUC of all active abemaciclib species by 70% and 29%–53%, respectively.^13^

DDIs are an especially important consideration for the treatment of cancer, with most therapies possessing narrow therapeutic indexes and steep dose-response curves.^16^ Although the impact of DDIs and pharmacogenetics are routinely assessed during clinical development, it is not feasible to study all possible interactions in dedicated clinical trials. More recently, the potential for complex interactions between patient genetics and concomitant medications has become more apparent^17–19^; consequently, the acceptance of PBPK models as a means to evaluate these complex interactions, including drug-drug-gene interactions (DDGIs), has grown.^20–22^ The objective of this study was to assess the DDGI potential of belzutifan when coadministered with abemaciclib and palbociclib in individuals with varying CYP2C19/UGT2B17 phenotypes using PBPK modeling.

## METHODS

PBPK modeling was conducted in Simcyp v.21 using a stepwise approach to assess the DDGI potential of belzutifan (perpetrator) on CDK 4/6 inhibitors (victims) in individuals with varying CYP2C19/UGT2B17 phenotypes. This stepwise approach is outlined in [Figure 1](#F1). First, a PBPK base model for belzutifan was constructed using input parameters predicted by Simcyp, calculated on the basis of clinical data, or provided in the FDA Multi-Discipline Review document for belzutifan using WebPlotDigitizer v5.^12^ Base model parameters were validated using the pharmacokinetic parameters maximum concentration (C_max_) and steady-state AUC during the dosing interval (AUC_0-τ_) for individuals in the 120 mg once daily dosing cohort from the phase I trial (MK-6482–001).^23^ Second, base model parameters were used to simulate belzutifan AUC_0-τ_ in individuals with varying CYP2C19 and UGT2B17 phenotypes and compared with population (PopPK/PGx) model estimates, as well as the sponsor’s original PBPK predictions. Third, using an identical trial design as the CYP3A clinical DDI study with midazolam (MK-6482–009),^7^ exposure of a single dose of midazolam (2 mg) was simulated in healthy volunteers alone and after 7 days of belzutifan (120 mg once daily), which was compared with trial results (AUCR = 0.60). Previously developed PBPK models for abemaciclib^15^ and palbociclib^24^ were revalidated in Simcyp v.21 (Certara, Inc; Princeton, NJ) by comparing current model predictions (v.21) with that of the originally observed and simulated data (v.14). Finally, virtual trials assessing the AUC from time 0 to infinity (AUC_0-inf_) of single-dose abemaciclib (200 mg) or palbociclib (125 mg) alone on day 1 and after 7 days of belzutifan (120 mg once daily) were simulated in healthy volunteers with differing CYP2C19 and UGT2B17 phenotypes. A more detailed description of each step is outlined in the [supplemental methods](#SD1).

## RESULTS

### Validation of Belzutifan Base PBPK Model

In accordance with the model included in the sponsor’s new drug application,^7^ the belzutifan base model consisted of a two-compartment, minimal PBPK model with first-order absorption. The belzutifan base model input parameters, along with their references or methods of estimation, are presented in the [Data Supplement (Table S1)](#SD1). The Simcyp NEurCaucasian population was adjusted to match the demographic characteristics (White race: 90.5%; age: 27–84 years; female: 26.3%) and frequencies of CYP2C19 and UGT2B17 phenotypes of patients enrolled in the MK-6482–001 trial. The accuracy of the model was verified by comparing steady-state pharmacokinetic parameters from a virtual clinical trial of 100 patients (10 × 10 design) treated with belzutifan 120 mg once daily for 15 days with observed week 3 data from the 120 mg once daily cohort of MK-6482–001.^23^ As shown in [Table 1](#T1), the fold-error of model-predicted C_max_ and AUC_0-τ_ values were 1.14 and 0.98, respectively.

### Validation of Model-Predicted Alterations in Belzutifan Exposure on the Basis of CYP2C19 and UGT2B17 Phenotypes

To simulate changes in belzutifan exposure in patients with reduced-function CYP2C19 and UGT2B17 phenotypes, the NEurCaucasian population in Simcyp was modified to match the demographic parameters (age: 19–84 years; percent females: 56.1%) of patients enrolled in the sponsor’s clinical trial population that informed their PopPK and PGx-PK models. Then, CYP2C19 and UGT2B17 phenotypes were adjusted within four separate virtual clinical trials to assess exposure of belzutifan 120 mg once daily for 15 days in the following CYP2C19/UGT2B17 phenotype groups: EM/EM, EM/PM, PM/EM, and PM/PM. [Table 2](#T2) shows the predicted AUC_0-τ_ for each phenotype group in comparison with the sponsor’s PopPK and PGx-PK model estimates and PBPK predictions. The exposure of belzutifan was predicted to increase approximately 5-fold in dual CYP2C19/UGT2B17 PMs relative to dual EMs, which was consistent with findings from the sponsor’s modeling analyses.

### Validation of the Belzutifan CYP3A4 Induction Model

Belzutifan CYP3A4 induction parameters, informed by the sponsor’s in vitro experiments and refined on the basis of results from a clinical DDI trial assessing the effect of belzutifan on midazolam (MK6482–009), were added to the belzutifan compound file. The Simcyp healthy volunteers population was adjusted to match the demographics (age: 20–50 years; percent females: 100%) of MK6482–009, and a virtual clinical trial was simulated in 100 individuals (10 × 10 design) that administered a single dose of 2 mg midazolam on day 1 and again on day 8, after administration of belzutifan 120 mg once daily on days 2–8. An identical virtual clinical trial was also simulated in the modified healthy volunteers population with the CYP2C19 and UGT2B17 phenotype frequencies adjusted to only include dual PMs. As shown in [Table 3](#T3), the model-predicted midazolam AUCR was 0.73 in the modified healthy volunteer population and 0.50 in the dual PM population; these estimated changes in midazolam AUCR were less than those predicted by the sponsor’s model predictions (AUCR of 0.62 and 0.37, respectively).

### Validation of Base Models for Palbociclib and Abemaciclib

Previously validated PBPK models for palbociclib^24^ and abemaciclib^15^ were redeveloped in Simcyp v.21. The palbociclib model included only the parent drug (input parameters provided in the [Data Supplement, Table S2](#SD1)), while the abemaciclib model included the parent drug (input parameters provided in the [Data Supplement, Table S3](#SD1)) and three active metabolites: M2, M18, and M20; input parameters for abemaciclib active metabolites are provided in the [Data Supplement (Tables S4](#SD1)–[S6](#SD1)). ### Predicted Alterations in CDK 4/6 Inhibitor Exposure in Combination With Belzutifan

To assess the potential for belzutifan to alter the exposure of palbociclib and abemaciclib, virtual clinical trials were simulated in 100 individuals (10 × 10 design) administered palbociclib 125 mg or abemaciclib 200 mg on days 1 and 8 along with belzutifan 120 mg once daily on days 2–8. Trials were simulated in individuals with CYP2C19/UGT2B17 EM/EM and PM/PM phenotypes. Predicted AUC_0-inf_ values for palbociclib, abemaciclib, and active metabolites in CYP2C19/UGT2B17 dual EMs and PMs are presented in [Table 4](#T4) with AUC_0-inf_ GMRs for each phenotype group during coadministration with belzutifan shown in [Figure 2](#F2). Compared with dual EMs, belzutifan exposure increased approximately 4.4-fold in dual PMs. Belzutifan decreased the AUC_0-inf_ of palbociclib by 13.2% and 36.8% in dual EMs and dual PMs, respectively. Similarly, belzutifan decreased the AUC_0-inf_ of abemaciclib by 24.3% and 52.2% in dual EMs and dual PMs, respectively, with the relative potency-adjusted unbound AUC_0-inf_ (as described in Posada et al)^15^ decreasing by 23.5% and 47.9%.

## DISCUSSION

In the present analysis, we assessed the DDGI potential of belzutifan when coadministered with the CDK 4/6 inhibitors abemaciclib and palbociclib in individuals with varying CYP2C19 and UGT2B17 phenotypes through PBPK modeling. In CYP2C19/UGT2B17 dual PMs, belzutifan exposure increased approximately 4.4-fold, and the exposure of palbociclib and abemaciclib decreased by approximately 37% and approximately 52%, respectively. The extent of his interaction in dual PMs is consistent with previous simulations described by Yu et al^24^ and Posada et al^15^ when administering either palbociclib or abemaciclib concomitantly with other moderate CYP3A inducers. Although belzutifan is described as a weak inducer of CYP3A in the FDA drug label,^2^ it should be emphasized that this may not be the case for all individuals. Belzutifan concentrations, and subsequently, its induction potential are highly variable, with the polymorphic enzymes CYP2C19 and UGT2B17 accounting for roughly 80% of its total oral clearance in dual EMs. This was evident in the phase II clinical study MK6482–004, where despite a primarily Caucasian-based population, belzutifan AUC varied approximately 8.5 fold^7^; similarly, a clinical DDI study with belzutifan and midazolam reported a 19.2% intrasubject coefficient of variation in midazolam AUCR, albeit in a limited number of patients.^7^ Our simulations confirm the potential for greater CYP3A induction in patients with reduced belzutifan metabolism since belzutifan was demonstrated to be a moderate inducer of abemaciclib in CYP2C19/UGT2B17 dual PMs. Belzutifan’s potential for moderate CYP3A induction in dual PMs is likely clinically significant when used in combination with abemaciclib, since the current abemaciclib drug label recommends avoiding concomitant use with moderate CYP3A inducers.^13^

Although not directly investigated in clinical studies with belzutifan, ethnicity plays a key role in determining which populations are most susceptible to the investigated DDGI. Although CYP2C19/UGT2B17 dual PMs are a rare occurrence in Caucasians (0.4%), up to 15% of certain Asian populations are expected to be dual PMs.^2^ Furthermore, given the rarity of dual PMs in Caucasians, it is unlikely that the investigated DDGI will be evaluated in ongoing belzutifan-CDK 4/6 inhibitor combination trials since these studies are being conducted at US sites likely to enroll predominantly Caucasian patients. For Caucasians, a similar and more common interaction would be the use of a strong CYP2C19 inhibitor (eg, fluvoxamine) in a patient with a UGT2B17 PM phenotype (occurring in approximately 15% of Caucasians).^2^ Lack of heterogeneity, including a diverse representation of patient genetics and polypharmacy in clinical trials, is well established,^25^ and it is not feasible to assess the infinite combinations of patient genetics, comorbid diseases, and concomitant medications that may affect real-world outcomes. Thus, PBPK modeling offers an efficient and accurate method to explore these types of complex interactions.^22^ An additional source of variability in belzutifan’s CYP3A induction potential is genetic variability in *CYP3A4* and *CYP3A5* conferring reduced enzyme function, as occurs in up to 5% and 52% to 93% of populations, respectively, on the basis of genomic ancestry.^26,27^ In patients with decreased or no function *CYP3A4* or *CYP3A5* alleles, the effect of belzutifan’s CYP3A induction would theoretically be reduced, as would the baseline metabolism of CYP3A substrates in the absence of belzutifan.

Despite its high interpatient variability in exposure, belzutifan is generally well tolerated, with most adverse events less severe than Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events Grade 3 and able to be managed with supportive care.^2,7^ As such, when used as monotherapy, no preemptive screening recommendations exist for *CYP2C19* or *UGT2B17* genetic testing before initiating treatment.^2^ Therefore, although toxicity risk alone is not sufficient to warrant preemptive screening for belzutifan, genetic variability in belzutifan metabolism may become an important consideration when administering belzutifan in combination with CYP3A substrates. Additionally, while the current analysis only assesses the effect of belzutifan on CDK 4/6 inhibitors, interactions with other CYP3A substrates, including other anticancer agents that could be used in combination with belzutifan (eg, cabozantinib),^28,29^ should also be explored.

A major limitation to the current analysis is the inability to fully appreciate the clinical significance of the investigated DDGI. That is, in dual PMs, does belzutifan at approximately 4 times higher exposure and abemaciclib at half the exposure affect treatment outcomes? This question is challenging to answer, given numerous uncertainties, most principally the potential for additive or synergistic pharmacodynamic effects when belzutifan is used in combination with CDK 4/6 inhibitors. In general, a better understanding of exposure-response (E-R) relationships for these therapies is needed, since only abemaciclib has a clearly defined E-R relationship.^30^ For CDK 4/6 inhibitors, a similar DDI may occur with concomitant proton pump inhibitor administration, which reduces CDK 4/6 inhibitor exposure and has been demonstrated to reduce progression-free survival rates in patients with breast cancer.^31,32^ Nonetheless, an approximately 30%–50% drop in palbociclib or abemaciclib exposure is significant on the basis of the FDA’s pharmacokinetic bioequivalence criteria (AUCR range of 0.8–1.25) and the abemaciclib FDA label recommendation to avoid co-administration with moderate CYP3A inducers, suggesting that DDI potential should be an important consideration when evaluating CYP3A substrates in combination with belzutifan.

Limitations also exist in using a traditional drug interaction trial design to properly assess the impact of this DDGI. Because of constraints of using publicly available data, both the belzutifan CYP3A4 induction model and CDK 4/6 inhibitor models were validated using single-dose administration and exposure (AUC_0-inf_) data when administering CYP3A substrates (midazolam, abemaciclib, or palbociclib) in the presence of inhibitors or inducers. Thus, we used an identical virtual trial design to assess the impact of belzutifan on CDK 4/6 substrates. Although this approach allows accurate estimation of the effect size of this DDGI after a single dose of the CYP3A substrates, the therapeutic effect and clinical outcome will be determined by exposure of both drugs at steady state, given that they are dosed daily.

Finally, the underprediction of the midazolam CYP3A clinical interaction should also be emphasized, despite validating against the same clinical data (MK6482–009) from which induction parameters were refined.^7^ Potential causes include differences between the authors’ and sponsor’s midazolam compound files, alterations made to the CYP3A4 turnover rate, and variances between the simulated and observed populations (ie, healthy volunteers *v* healthy women of nonchildbearing potential). Regardless, this seems to be an isolated issue, given the good concordance between the authors’ predicted belzutifan exposure (17.1 ng × h/mL) with the observed (23.4 ng × h/mL) and applicant simulated values (16.9 ng × h/mL).^7^ Conversely, it is possible that the limited sample size (N = 13) observed in the midazolam clinical study (MK6482–009) overstates the extent of the interaction, especially given belzutifan’s variable exposure and induction characteristics. Furthermore, despite this underprediction, the simulated midazolam AUCR (0.73) still lies close to the 90% CI (0.53 to 0.69) of the observed interaction (0.60).^7^

In summary, we assessed the DDGI potential of belzutifan in combination with CDK 4/6 inhibitors in individuals with varying CYP2C19 and UGT2B17 phenotypes using the best available clinical evidence. Our predictions found that belzutifan reduced CDK 4/6 inhibitor exposure, with greater reductions in CYP2C19/UGT2B17 dual PMs. The clinical significance of these interactions warrants further investigation.

### Key Objective

Our study objective was to assess the clinical relevance of drug-drug interactions between belzutifan and the cyclin-dependent kinase 4/6 inhibitors abemaciclib and palbociclib, including in patients with varying *CYP2C19* and *UGT2B17* genotype-predicted phenotypes, using physiologically based pharmacokinetic modeling.

### Knowledge Generated

Our simulations demonstrate that, in virtual clinical trials with CYP2C19 and UGT2B17 normal metabolizers, belzutifan reduced the exposure of abemaciclib plus its active metabolites and palbociclib by 23.5% and 13.2%, respectively. By contrast, belzutifan reduced the predicted exposure of abemaciclib plus its active metabolites and palbociclib by 47.9% and 36.8%, respectively, in CYP2C19 and UGT2B17 dual poor metabolizers.

### Relevance

Our simulations identify the potential for clinically significant drug-drug interactions for belzutifan with abemaciclib and palbociclib, with the severity based on CYP2C19 and UGT2B17 phenotypes; these findings warrant further investigation since belzutifan is currently being studied in clinical trials in combination with abemaciclib and palbociclib.
