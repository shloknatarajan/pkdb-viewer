## Abstract

**Background:** 
Elexacaftor/tezacaftor/ivacaftor (ETI) triple therapy has been introduced as causal therapy for people with cystic fibrosis (pwCF) carrying at least one F508del allele. Data on the real-world effect of ETI in pwCF without any F508del allele are limited.

**Methods:** 
In this observational cohort study, data from the German CF Registry for pwCF who received off-label ETI for up to 365 days were used to study effects on lung function, nutritional status and sweat chloride concentration (SCC).

**Results:** 
616 pwCF (mean±sd age 28.8±13.9 years) were registered with ETI treatment within compassionate use (n=509; at least one F508del allele present) or off-label use (carrying non-F508del mutations likely to respond to ETI (n=81) or not (n=26)). After 1 year, mean percentage predicted forced expiratory volume in 1 s increased by 10.3±11.0 (p<0.0001) and 9.0±10.7 (p<0.0001) percentage points compared to baseline in the at least one F508del and non-F508del predicted responsive groups, and remained stable in the non-F508del predicted non-responsive group (p=0.31). Body mass index percentile increased in the at least one F508del and non-F508del predicted responsive groups (11.4±15.2; p<0.0001 and 5.0±12.3; p<0.05, respectively), and remained stable in the non-F508del predicted non-responsive group (p=0.14). Mean SCC after 3 months decreased by 43.9±24.6 mmol·L−1 (p<0.0001) and 34.4±22.9 mmol·L−1 (p<0.0001), respectively, in the at least one F508del and non-F508del predicted responsive groups, and increased in the non-F508del predicted non-responsive group (14.8±9.5 mmol·L−1; p<0.05).

**Conclusions:** 
Real-world data align with the results of randomised clinical trials for pwCF carrying at least one F508del allele. Clinical improvements in pwCF with a range of CF transmembrane conductance regulator mutations treated off-label with ETI provide compelling support for the recent ETI label extension in Europe.

## Introduction

Cystic fibrosis (CF) is a life-limiting, multisystem, autosomal recessive disease affecting over 7000 individuals in Germany and about 100 000 worldwide [[1](#C1)–[4](#C4)]. Mutations in the CF transmembrane conductance regulator (*CFTR*) gene cause symptoms across multiple organs, although pulmonary disease remains the leading cause of morbidity and mortality [[1](#C1), [5](#C5)]. In Germany, advances in therapy have increased the median survival age of people with CF (pwCF) to 66.7 years, with adults outnumbering children since 2008 [[2](#C2)]. The current standard therapy for eligible pwCF involves CFTR modulators: potentiators (*e.g.* ivacaftor) and correctors (*e.g.* elexacaftor, tezacaftor and lumacaftor). Potentiators are effective in pwCF with gating or residual function mutations, while those with the most common mutation, *F508del*, require a combination of at least one corrector and a potentiator. In Europe, elexacaftor/tezacaftor/ivacaftor (ETI) triple therapy has been approved since August 2020 for pwCF aged ≥12 years who are homozygous for *F508del* or compound heterozygous with a minimal function mutation. Approval has since expanded to all pwCF aged ≥12 years with at least one *F508del* allele, and later to children aged 6–11 and 2–5 years. These modulators restore CFTR function and improve lung function, nutrition and quality of life, while reducing sweat chloride concentration (SCC) and pulmonary exacerbations in pwCF with at least one *F508del* allele [[6](#C6)–[10](#C10)]. Pre-clinical studies also showed ETI efficacy in other *CFTR* mutations, leading the US Food and Drug Administration (FDA) to extend its label [[11](#C11)]. Initial studies and case reports have demonstrated ETI effectiveness in pwCF without *F508del* but with at least one mutation allowing CFTR protein production, suggesting responsiveness to correction and potentiation [[12](#C12), [13](#C13)]. Recently, the European Medicines Agency extended ETI approval to all pwCF aged ≥2 years with at least one non-class I *CFTR* mutation. Although ETI has been widely used in pwCF with *F508del* since its German approval in August 2020, real-world data on its off-label use in pwCF with non-class I mutations remain scarce. Using data from the German CF Registry of Mukoviszidose e.V. [[2](#C2)], this observational cohort study investigates the extent and clinical impact of ETI therapy in a broad cohort of pwCF with diverse *CFTR* mutations.

## Methods

### Study design, population and treatment

This study analysed encounter-based documentation from the German CF Registry [[14](#C14)] between 1 January 2018 and 8 December 2023. Written informed consent was obtained from all participants or, for minors, from their legal guardian or closest relative. PwCF with a history of organ transplantation were excluded. In the context of ETI therapy, treatment interruptions of up to 30 days were considered continuous therapy. Of the 69 centres with encounter-based documentation, all pwCF treated off-label with ETI before or without regulatory approval were included, representing 64 centres. The cohort consisted of three groups ([figure 1](#F1)): 1) pwCF with at least one *F508del* allele treated with ETI within a clinical trial (open-label phase), through a compassionate use programme or as an individual healing trial before official approval (“at least one *F508del*”); 2) pwCF without any *F508del* allele but with at least one *CFTR* mutation likely responsive to ETI due to CFTR protein production (“non-*F508del* predicted responsive”) [[11](#C11), [12](#C12), [15](#C15)]; and 3) pwCF without any *F508del* allele and without a mutation expected to respond to ETI due to lack of CFTR protein production (“non-*F508del* predicted non-responsive”) [[12](#C12), [15](#C15)]. The non-F508del predicted responsive and non-responsive groups represent strictly off-label use. In the at least one *F508del* group, the off-label period ended upon regulatory approval for the specific genotype and age group. In the non-*F508del* predicted responsive and non-responsive groups, ETI was considered ongoing in the absence of an end date. Within the non-*F508del* predicted responsive group, subgroup analyses were conducted: 1) “FDA approved” (pwCF with at least one *CFTR* mutation approved by the FDA (mutation list in [supplementary methods](https://publications.ersnet.org/lookup/doi/10.1183/23120541.00667-2025#supplementary))) and 2) “clinical response no FDA” (pwCF without an FDA-approved mutation, but with at least one responsive mutation identified in the French compassionate use programme or recent literature [[12](#C12), [15](#C15)] (mutation list in [supplementary methods](https://publications.ersnet.org/lookup/doi/10.1183/23120541.00667-2025#supplementary))). Further, the clinical response no FDA group was subdivided into: 1) “clinical response no FDA, *N1303K*-positive” (pwCF with at least one *N1303K* allele) and 2) “clinical response no FDA, *N1303K*-negative” (pwCF without an *N1303K* allele).

### Study parameters

Data were collected on lung function and body mass index (BMI), with lung function assessed as percentage predicted forced expiratory volume in 1 s (FEV_1_), measured in accordance with American Thoracic Society/European Respiratory Society standards and analysed using Global Lung Function Initiative 2012 reference equations for Caucasians [[16](#C16)]. BMI was documented as percentiles [[17](#C17)–[19](#C19)]. SCC levels were recorded at least once within the year prior to ETI initiation and during the first year of ETI therapy. Pulmonary exacerbations were defined as the need for additional oral, inhaled or intravenous antibiotics, accompanied by at least two of the following criteria: change in sputum volume or colour; increased cough; fatigue, malaise or lethargy; anorexia or weight loss; ≥10% decline in lung function; radiographic abnormalities; and/or increased dyspnoea [[20](#C20)]. Data collected during a pulmonary exacerbation or during ETI treatment interruptions exceeding 30 days were excluded from the analysis. All antibiotic treatments, including additions to ongoing chronic therapy, are recorded in the German CF Registry, where indications for non-chronic antibiotic use are selected from a predefined list. Only antibiotic therapies explicitly prescribed for pulmonary exacerbations were considered in documentation and analysis. A response to ETI was defined by meeting at least one of the following criteria: 1) FEV_1_ % pred increase of >5%; 2) initial underweight status (BMI <18.5 kg·m^−2^, z-score < –3) and BMI gain of ≥1 kg·m^−2^ in adults or z-score increase of ≥0.25 in children and adolescents; and 4) median reduction in SCC of >20 mmol·L^−1^.

### Statistical analysis

Baseline demographics and clinical characteristics were described using means with standard deviations or proportions. Since registry data were collected at varying times and intervals, standardised quarterly periods were created for analysis. Mean values of FEV_1_ % pred, BMI percentile and SCC were calculated for each quarter, starting before ETI initiation and continuing through four quarters of treatment. When follow-up data were limited, the last quarter with a representative sample was used; values from smaller samples are shown in brackets. Pulmonary exacerbations and treatment responses were recorded dichotomously (yes/no) for one quarter prior to and four quarters following ETI initiation. Additionally, pulmonary exacerbation rates per patient-year were calculated. Paired comparisons across quarters used the paired t-test or McNemar's test. Pulmonary exacerbation rates were assessed *via* Poisson regression with an offset for patient-years. Between-group differences were analysed using the independent t-test, F-test and Chi-squared test. To evaluate group differences over time, a mixed models for repeated measures (MMRM) model with an autoregressive covariance structure was applied to FEV_1_ % pred, BMI percentile and SCC. Fixed effects included group, quarter, their interaction, baseline value and its interaction with quarter. All p-values were considered exploratory. Statistical analyses were performed using SAS version 9.4 (SAS Institute, Cary, NC, USA).

## Results

### Study population and follow-up

Of 7710 pwCF in the registry dataset, 616 (7.8%) received ETI before/without its regulatory approval in Europe between 1 January 2018 and 8 December 2023 ([figure 1](#F1) and [table 1](#TB1)). The number of pwCF who had data available for analysis differed between parameters ([table 1](#TB1)). The cohort consisted of 509 pwCF with at least one *F508del* allele treated with ETI before official approval (at least one *F508del* group), 81 pwCF in the non-*F508del* predicted responsive group and 26 pwCF in the non-*F508del* predicted non-responsive group ([figure 1](#F1) and [table 1](#TB1)). The mean age of the cohort was 28.8±13.9 years (range 1.0–72.0 years; 78.1% adults). Comparison between the groups revealed a higher mean age in the non-*F508del* predicted responsive group compared to the at least one *F508del* group (+6.5 years) and the non-*F508del* predicted non-responsive group (+7.2 years) ([table 1](#TB1)). There was an equal sex distribution in the cohort, while the *CFTR* genotype composition of the groups differed by definition ([table 1](#TB1) and [supplementary table S1](https://publications.ersnet.org/lookup/doi/10.1183/23120541.00667-2025#supplementary)). Pancreatic sufficiency was more prevalent in the non-*F508del* predicted responsive group than in the at least one *F508del* group (26.9% *versus* 6.3%) ([table 1](#TB1)). Mean BMI was slightly higher in the non-*F508del* predicted responsive group compared to the at least one *F508del* group (20.9±3.9 *versus* 19.6±3.5 kg·m^−2^) despite no difference in BMI percentile between all three groups ([table 1](#TB1)). There was no difference in the frequency of pancreatic insufficiency or BMI between the at least one *F508del* and non-*F508del* predicted non-responsive groups or the non-*F508del* predicted responsive and the non-responsive groups at baseline. Mean FEV_1_ % pred and percentage of pwCF experiencing pulmonary exacerbations and with chronic detection of *Pseudomonas aeruginosa* were comparable between groups ([table 1](#TB1)). Prevalence of CF-related diabetes was 29.3% in the cohort and comparable between groups, while prevalence of CF-related liver disease was slightly higher in the non-*F508del* predicted non-responsive group compared to the at least one *F508del* group ([table 1](#TB1)). Baseline SCC tended to be lower in the non-*F508del* predicted non-responsive group compared to the at least one *F508del* group, despite pre-treatment with a CFTR modulator in about half of the at least one *F508del* group ([table 1](#TB1)).

### Impact of off-label ETI therapy on CF lung disease

In pwCF in the at least one *F508del* group, there was a significant and consistent improvement from baseline in mean FEV_1_ % pred (10.3±11.0 percentage points), and a reduction in the proportion experiencing pulmonary exacerbations and in the pulmonary exacerbation rate after initiation of ETI therapy ([table 2](#TB2), and [figures 2](#F2) and [3](#F3)). A comparable improvement could be observed in the non-*F508del* predicted responsive group (FEV_1_ % pred 9.0±10.7 percentage points), while the non-*F508del* predicted non-responsive group remained stable throughout follow-up ([table 2](#TB2) and [figure 2](#F2)). Analysis of responsive subgroups (see [supplementary table S2](https://publications.ersnet.org/lookup/doi/10.1183/23120541.00667-2025#supplementary) for baseline characteristics) revealed a sustained improvement in FEV_1_ % pred from baseline throughout the observation period in the pwCF with FDA approved, non-*F508del* mutations (FDA approved group; 10.4±10.6 percentage points) ([table 3](#TB3)). The percentage of pwCF of this subgroup experiencing a pulmonary exacerbation tended to decline after initiation of ETI, as did the exacerbation rate ([table 3](#TB3)). Comparably, FEV_1_ % pred increased in pwCF of the clinical response no FDA subgroup (7.0±10.8 percentage points), while the proportion of pwCF experiencing a pulmonary exacerbation remained stable in this subgroup despite a reduction in the exacerbation rate ([table 3](#TB3)). The clinical response no FDA, *N1303K*-positive (pwCF with at least one *N1303K* allele) subgroup showed a strong improvement in FEV_1_ % pred (14.6±7.5 percentage points) and a tendency to a lower proportion experiencing a pulmonary exacerbation with a reduction of the exacerbation rate by 86.0% ([table 3](#TB3)). The FEV_1_ % pred in the clinical response no FDA*, N1303K*-negative (pwCF without an *N1303K* allele) subgroup remained stable, as did the proportion of pwCF experiencing a pulmonary exacerbation and the exacerbation rate ([table 3](#TB3)).

### Comparisons of responders to ETI therapy according to respective group

A predefined response to ETI was present in 71.4% of the at least one *F508del* group and in 53.9% of the non-*F508del* predicted responsive group after 1 year of ETI therapy, while such a response was present in 16.7% of the non-*F508del* predicted non-responsive group after 9 months ([table 2](#TB2)). Of note, there was no difference in baseline characteristics between responders and non-responders in the non-*F508del* predicted responsive group besides a slightly higher proportion with CFTR modulator pre-treatment in the non-responders ([supplementary table S3](https://publications.ersnet.org/lookup/doi/10.1183/23120541.00667-2025#supplementary)). A subgroup analysis of the non-*F508del* predicted responsive group showed that 58.3% of pwCF of the FDA approved subgroup had a response after 1 year ([table 3](#TB3)). Comparably, 46.7% of the pwCF of the clinical response no FDA subgroup fulfilled the response criteria, and within this subgroup, 71.4% of the pwCF with at least one *N1303K* allele (clinical response no FDA, *N1303K*-positive) had a response to ETI, while the group without an *N1303K* allele (clinical response no FDA, *N1303K*-negative) was too small for response analysis ([table 3](#TB3)).

### Longitudinal comparison between treatment groups

Comparisons using MMRM models showed significantly higher adjusted means for FEV_1_ % pred for the at least one *F508del* group compared to the non-*F508del* predicted non-responsive group in Quarter 1 (p<0.05) and Quarter 2 (p<0.01) as well as for the non-*F508del* predicted responsive group compared to the non-responsive group in Quarter 1 (p<0.01) and Quarter 2 (p<0.01). No difference was found between the at least one *F508del* and the non-*F508del* predicted responsive group in any quarter after initiation of ETI ([supplementary table S4](https://publications.ersnet.org/lookup/doi/10.1183/23120541.00667-2025#supplementary)). Regarding BMI percentile, MMRM comparison showed significantly higher adjusted means for the at least one *F508del* group in Quarter 2 (p<0.05) and Quarter 3 (p<0.01) compared to the non-*F508del* predicted non-responsive group. Also, adjusted means for BMI percentiles were significantly higher in the at least one *F508del* group than in the non-*F508del* predicted responsive group in Quarter 3 (p<0.01) and Quarter 4 (p<0.05), but not Quarter 1 and Quarter 2. No difference in any quarter was detected between the non-*F508del* predicted responsive and non-responsive groups ([supplementary table S4](https://publications.ersnet.org/lookup/doi/10.1183/23120541.00667-2025#supplementary)). For SCC, pwCF in the at least one *F508del* group showed significantly lower adjusted means compared to the non-*F508del* predicted non-responsive group in Quarter 1 (p<0.001) and Quarter 2 (p<0.001). When comparing SCC between the at least one *F508del* group and the non-*F508del* predicted responsive group, the adjusted means were significantly lower in the at least one *F508del* group than in the non-*F508del* predicted responsive group in Quarter 1 (p<0.05), Quarter 2 (p<0.01) and Quarter 4 (p<0.05). Comparisons between the non-*F508del* predicted responsive and non-responsive groups were not possible due to low numbers ([supplementary table S4](https://publications.ersnet.org/lookup/doi/10.1183/23120541.00667-2025#supplementary)).

## Discussion

This is the first study using real-world data from the German CF Registry to assess the treatment effects of an off-label therapy with ETI before/without regulatory approval in pwCF of almost all ages. The findings of this registry-based, observational study showed statistically significant improvements in clinical outcomes up to 12 months after initiation of ETI, including improved FEV_1_ % pred, increased BMI percentile, decreased SCC and a reduction in the number of pwCF experiencing pulmonary exacerbations as well as the pulmonary exacerbation rate in both pwCF with at least one *F508del* allele (at least one *F508del* group) and those carrying at least one *CFTR* mutation known or likely to respond to ETI treatment (non-*F508del* predicted responsive group). There were no detectable improvements following initiation of ETI in the group of pwCF with *CFTR* mutations known to be associated with no protein production (non-*F508del* predicted non-responsive group).

Changes in lung function, pulmonary exacerbation prevalence and rate, nutritional status, and SCC in the at least one *F508del* and non-*F508del* predicted responsive groups aligned with findings from randomised controlled trials and real-world studies in specific pwCF cohorts treated with ETI [[6](#C6)–[10](#C10), [12](#C12), [15](#C15), [16](#C16), [21](#C21)–[24](#C24)]. This consistency supports the quality of data from the German CF Registry and highlights the value of real-world evidence. Registries have significantly advanced the understanding of CF pathogenesis, its health impact and therapy outcomes through benchmarking. With advancements in big data analytics, registries can now be integrated with other data sources to better assess treatment effectiveness in real-world settings [[25](#C25)–[27](#C27)].

Our cohort included various groups of pwCF treated with ETI before or without approval. The largest was the at least one *F508del* group, comprising pwCF treated mostly within a compassionate use programme. While similar in age to those in clinical trials, their mean baseline FEV_1_ % pred was lower and ranged widely from 11.0% to 116.0% [[6](#C6), [8](#C8), [9](#C9)], indicating greater disease severity. Many in this group would have been excluded from pivotal trials due to FEV_1_ % pred <40%, while others with normal lung function (FEV_1_ % pred >90%) might also have been excluded. Thus, the at least one *F508del* group reflects the full spectrum of CF severity, although on average more severely affected than the general German CF population [[2](#C2)]. Despite this, treatment effects in this group were consistent with findings from clinical trials and real-world studies [[6](#C6)–[10](#C10), [12](#C12), [15](#C15), [16](#C16), [21](#C21)–[24](#C24)]. Notably, the proportion of responders ranged from 64.2% to 71.4% in the year following ETI initiation, suggesting some pwCF did not meet response criteria at all time-points. This aligns with pivotal trial data, where a subset of participants did not achieve a FEV_1_ % pred improvement >5% [[9](#C9)].

Interestingly, the effects of ETI on lung function, BMI and SCC were comparable between the at least one *F508del* and non-*F508del* predicted responsive groups ([table 2](#TB2)). This aligns with FDA approval of ETI for additional *CFTR* mutations based on *in vitro* data [[11](#C11), [28](#C28)], and with studies showing clinical benefit in pwCF carrying these and other likely responsive mutations [[12](#C12), [15](#C15), [29](#C29)]. Our analysis of the German CF Registry further supports evidence that pwCF with the *N1303K* mutation respond to ETI ([table 3](#TB3)), despite this mutation falling below the manufacturer's predefined *in vitro* cut-off [[12](#C12), [13](#C13), [15](#C15), [24](#C24), [29](#C29)].

Findings in the non-*F508del* predicted non-responsive group show no consistent effect of ETI on FEV_1_ % pred, BMI or SCC in pwCF with *CFTR* mutations causing no protein production ([table 2](#TB2)). This group was small, representing only 4% of the cohort. While results align with previous studies in similar genotypes [[12](#C12), [15](#C15), [24](#C24)], they should be interpreted with caution. In selected cases, pre-clinical testing of ETI responsiveness, followed by clinical phenotyping if needed, may be beneficial.

As this study relies on data from the German CF Registry, results are limited to what the registry captures. Thus, the number of enrolled pwCF defines the scope of the study. Since the registry only documents pulmonary exacerbations requiring antibiotic treatment, milder episodes not needing intervention are excluded. Notably, the launch of compassionate use programmes and ETI approval coincided with COVID-19-related measures in Germany. Therefore, the observed reduction in pulmonary exacerbation rates cannot be solely attributed to ETI. Increased hygiene and contact restrictions may have contributed to reduced pulmonary exacerbation rates across all groups, including the non-*F508del* predicted non-responsive group.

In conclusion, these findings offer valuable insights into the real-world effects of off-label ETI use in pwCF in Germany. The data demonstrate that ETI therapy yields substantial clinical benefits in a heterogeneous group of pwCF, including those with at least one *F508del* allele or a *CFTR* mutation likely to respond to ETI. As such, our results contribute to the growing body of evidence on the efficacy of ETI in rare *CFTR* mutations, which are often under-represented in clinical trials and sparsely documented in case reports. The observed improvements in both clinical outcomes and CFTR function emphasise the importance of ensuring access to ETI for all pwCF with mutations shown to be responsive.
