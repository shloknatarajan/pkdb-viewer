What is actually in PK-DB?

Start with one pharmacokinetic fact, follow every link that gives it meaning, then zoom out to twenty real records and the database-wide counts.

Start with one specific observation

In the 1990 paper The effect of propranolol on paracetamol metabolism in man, PK-DB stores the following result in PKDB00024/study.json:

After a 1.5 g oral dose of paracetamol, a group of 10 healthy volunteers had a mean plasma elimination half-life of 3.4 ± 0.3 hours.

That sentence is assembled from four separate records. The output itself does not repeat the paper, cohort, or dose; it carries foreign keys that point to them.

The cohort record adds details that would otherwise be lost: ages 21–32 years, median weight 68 kg with a 51–85 kg range, overnight fasting, no regular medication, and no oral contraceptive use. The study also stores paracetamol metabolites, antipyrine, and propranolol-related experimental context rather than reducing the paper to one headline result.

The same experiment also becomes a curve

PK-DB stores a group-mean paracetamol plasma concentration series for the same study. It has 11 observations from 0.5 to 24 hours, each with a mean and SD. The database value is normalized to grams per liter; 0.0262 g/L is 26.2 mg/L.

What PK-DB contributes: not merely “paracetamol half-life = 3.4 h,” but the publication provenance, subject definition, dose, route, tissue, statistic, variability, normalized unit, related metabolites, and—when available—the underlying concentration trajectory.

Substances are an ontology, not just a drug list

A substance node has a stable identifier and display label. It may describe what was administered, what was measured, a metabolite, an endogenous biomarker, or even a dietary interaction.

4Substance family

Groups and individuals describe who was measured

Groups carry cohort-level characteristics and may have parent/child structure. Individuals carry subject-level characteristics and can inherit context from a group. The word “individual” means a PK-DB entity, not necessarily a deduplicated participant across all extracted endpoints.

Do not read 1,093 “individuals” as 1,093 unique people. Some studies create multiple individual-linked entities for separately digitized endpoints, as the names F_obese, HLObese_1, and CLObese_3 illustrate. Likewise, group counts can overlap through parent/child or crossover designs. PK-DB preserves the source structure; it does not provide a globally deduplicated participant census.

The detailed snapshot contains 37 distinct output measurement types. Nearly half of all output rows are concentrations, because a curve may also be represented point-by-point. Scope, provenance, and how counts were made

Read this before reusing a numberThe repository contains two non-synchronized snapshots. Use the 803-study API catalog for broad study-level coverage. Use the 55-study 2021 export for examples, parameters, subject attributes, interventions, and actual result rows.
  • Detailed source: the 55 entries in app_data/pkdb_annotations/index.json and their checked-in study.json files. The upstream export is documented as having 56 open studies; ingestion skips one with no extracted entities.
  • Catalog source: all 803 records in pkdb-api/studies_full.json.
  • Unique concepts: substances and measurement types were keyed by their ontology SID, not display text.
  • Time-course points: counted only positions with a non-null time and a non-null individual value or mean.
  • Missingness: explicit nulls and “not reported” choices were retained. No values were inferred from the papers.
  • Linkage: all 19,494 detailed outputs link to an intervention and to either a group (10,726) or individual entity (8,768). Units are present on 19,459 outputs (99.8%).
Snapshot mismatch: only 35 of the 55 detailed study IDs occur in the 803-study catalog; 20 older IDs are absent. The files should be analyzed as separate snapshots, not force-joined into one supposedly current database state.
